Macbeth

Overview

Macbeth is the tragic hero of Shakespeare's play. He begins as a respected and courageous soldier, but his ambition and desire for power lead him towards violence and his eventual downfall.

Character development

Beginning of the play

Macbeth is presented as brave and loyal, earning praise for his actions in battle.

Middle of the play

Macbeth becomes increasingly consumed by ambition, commiting murder to secure his position as king.

End of the play

Macbeth becomes a tryant who has lost his morality and is eventually defeated.

Relationships

Lady Macbeth

Macbeth and Lady Macbeth initially have a strong partnership, with Lady Macbeth encouraging him to pursue his power. However, as the play progresses, Macbeth becomes more independant and ruthless.

Banquo

Banquo acts as a contrast to Macbeth. Whilst both recieve prophecies from the Witches, Banquo chooses to remain honourable, shwoing Macbeth's moral decline.

The Witches

The Witches influence Macbeth by introducing the prophecies that awaken his ambition. However Macbeth is responsible for the choices he makes afterwards.

Key Themes

  • Ambition
  • Power
  • Guilt
  • Supernatural

Key Quotes

"Vaulting ambition"

Theme: Ambition

Shows Macbeth recognising that his ambition is driving him towards destruction.

"Is this a dagger which I see before me?"

Theme: Supernatural / Guilt

Represents Macbeth's uncertainty and the influence of the supernatural.

"I am in blood, stepped in so far that, should I wade no more, returning were as tedious as go'er"

Theme: Guilt

Macbeth has reached a turning point, and has to make a decision between attempting salvation, or to carry on down the destructive path he is already walking down.

Exam tips

Link Macbeth to ambition

When writing about Macbeth, connect his actions to his overwhelming ambition and desire for power, and he allows his ambition to dictate his actions.

Discuss his change throughout the play

To achieve top marks, you need to speak about the play as a whole, this means comparing the noble warrior fighting for king and country at the beginning to the malicious tyrant at the end of the play and what causes this change.

Use context

Link Macbeth's downfall to Jacobean beliefs about kingship, the supernatural and the consequences of disrupting the natural order. Again to acheive top marks, showing you know and understand the context of the play is crucial to achieveing the top bands/marks on the mark scheme.
