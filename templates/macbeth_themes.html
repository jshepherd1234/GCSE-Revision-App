Macbeth Themes

Explore the main themes in Macbeth and how Shakespeare develops them.

Ambition

Ambition drives much of the play. Macbeth's desire for power grows after hearing the witches' prophecies, eventually leading him to betray his values and commit increasily violent and treacherous actions.

Key characters

  • Macbeth
  • Lady Macbeth
  • The Witches

Key quotations

  • "Vaulting ambition"
  • "Why do I yield to that horrid image that doth unfix my hair and make my seated heart knock at my ribs"

Guilt

Overview

Guilt affects both Macbeth and Lady Macbeth. It appears through fear, hallucinations, and distrubuted sleep, showing their psychological cost of their actions.

Key characters

  • Macbeth
  • Lady Macbeth

Key quotations

  • "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood clean from my hand?"
  • "I am in blood stepped in so far that, should I wade no more, returning were as tedious as go'er"

The supernatural

Overview

The supernatural creates uncertainty throughout the play. The witches, visions and apparitions influence Macbeth, but Shakespeare leaves open the question of whether they contorl him or merely tempt him.

Key characters

  • The Witches
  • Macbeth
  • Banquo

Key quotations

  • "Fair is foul, foul is fair"
  • "Heat oppressed brain"

Power and kingship

Overview

The play contrasts rightful kingship with tyranny. Duncan and Malcolm are linked with order and loyaly, while Macbeth rules with fear and violence.

Key characters

  • Duncan
  • Macbeth
  • Malcolm
  • Macduff

Key quotations

  • "Here lays Duncan, his silver skin laced with golden blood, his gashed stabs looking like a breach in nature"
  • "Bleed, bleed, pour country"
